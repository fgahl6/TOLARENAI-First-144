The Major Zodiac Constellations

Aires, the Ram: a A northern constellation usually named as the first sign in the zodiac into which, when the sun enters at the vernal equinox in March (celebrated as Easter) where the days and nights are of equal length. During many ages, Aries has been regarded by the devout as the celestial representative, visible in the heavens, of the Lamb of God that taketh away the sins of the world. Ancient calendars always began with the vernal equinox and the sign of Aires.
Taurus, the Bull: b The second sign in the zodiac. The ancient Egyptians worshiped this constellation as the heavenly representative of their god Osiris. It was likely so named by the Egyptians to designate that period of the year (April) in which cows mostly bring forth their young.
Gemini, the Twins: c The third sign in the zodiac is a constellation visible in May containing the two bright stars named Castor and Pollux. These two stars are the fabled sons of Leda and Jupiter, who cleared the Hellespont and neighboring seas of pirates during their lives. They were therefore deemed the protector of navigators and sailors. Gemini is considered part of spring, and goats give birth during this time, usually giving birth to two at a time.

Cancer, the Crab: d The fourth sign in the zodiac in which the sun enters on the 21st day of June which is the summer solstice. The name represents the apparent backward motion of the sun in June, which is said to resemble the motions of a crab.
Leo, the Lion: e Leo is the fifth sign in the zodiac and contains one star of the first magnitude, Regulus or Cor Leonis the Lions Heart. Leo is the star group, through, which the sun moved in July when its heat was most powerful, being compared to the most ferocious of the beasts. Leo is often portrayed as an enraged lion. In ancient times there were many feasts in honor of the sun.

Virgo, the Virgin Mother, Venus, Eve, Isis: f The sixth sign of the zodiac is when the sun enters about the 21st of August. There are many myths and fables regarding the virgin, which are among all nations and religions. They all agree that the female holds a branch of ripened wheat or fruit in her right hand. This is intended to represent the reproductive powers of nature, the abundance satisfaction that people enjoy during the happy period of harvest.
Libra, the balance: g The seventh sign of the zodiac, directly opposite to Aries, from which it is 180 degrees distant. Libra is marked after the manner of a scale. The scale denotes when the Sun arrives at the ecliptic where the days and nights are equal as if weighed in balance.
The Sun enters Libra on or near September 21st). This is called the autumn equinox. September 25th is when John the Baptist is born. John the Baptist is the forerunner of his cousin Jesus. Jesus comes to his exaltation of glory on March 24th, the Vernal Equinox at Easter. The equinoxes equally mark the births and deaths of John and Jesus. One advanced, the other declined. Jesus ascended; John descended. Astrologically speaking, He must increase, but I must decrease. John 3:20.
Scorpio, the Scorpion: h The eighth sign of the zodiac which the sun enters near the 23rd of October. Scorpio is fabled to have killed the great hunter Orion. For this reason, it is also said that when Scorpio rises, Orion sets.
In November in the northern hemisphere, when the sun enters Scorpio, it reaches a red star named Antares. It is one of only four red stars that the ancients noted in the night sky, the other being; Aldebaran, Arcturus, and Betelgeuse. All four were feared by the Ancients because of their different nature. In Christian mythology, Antares represents Judas Iscariot, who betrays Jesus and brings him down to his death. It is said that the sting of a scorpion often resembles the image of a kiss.
Sagittarius, the Archer: i The ninth zodiacal sign corresponds with November. This sign is represented like a centaur and was fabled to be Crotus, the son of Eupheme, the nurse of the Muses.
Sagittarius is the individual with the arrow. He is the one that inflicts punishment on the sun and who is there on the day the sun dies on December 21. It is also Sagittarius and, with his arrow, fatefully injures Achilles (representing the sun) heel as the Winter Solstice shows no mercy.
Capricorn, the Goat: j Capricorn is the tenth sign of the zodiac and occurs when the sun is near the winter solstice, December 21st (the longest night of the year). This sign represents a goat's horns, fabled to be Pan, who was taken to heaven in the shape of a goat in the war of the giants. Capricorn represents his motion after the manner of a goat climbing the mountains. The goat is Capricorn after the winter solstice, climbing as a new ascension begins.
Aquarius, the Water Bearer: k A constellation in the heavens is so-called because there is usually an abundance of rain during its rising. It is the eleventh sign in the zodiac. Aquarius rises in January and sets in February. January is often portrayed by Janus (two faces) looking both to the previous year and forward to the new year.

Pisces, the Fishes: l The twelfth sign of the zodiac rises in February. It is represented by the two fishes tied together by the tails. Fish indicates the fishing season in the northern hemisphere. The Greeks fabled these fishes to be those into which Venus and Cupid were changed to escape the giant Typhon. In Catholic churches, there is usually found a vase filled with water (called Piscina,) and this water is considered holy. The Fish-days are observed as holy days or fasting days in which fish may be eaten, and meat is forbidden.

The Constellations

Below is a list of other significant constellations. This list is fromThe Ruins, or Meditation on the Revolutions of Empires and the Law of Natureby Constantin Francois de Volney, first published under theFrench Citizens Catechism, 1793. The language for this edition has been updated for modern tastes. At the time of its writing, it was hoped that this information would become common to all Europe.

Sirius, the Dog Star: A bright star of the first magnitude in the mouth of the constellation Canis Major.This is the brightest star in our firmament and is one of the nearest.

Lepus:One of the southern constellations, placed near Orion, according to Grecian fable, because it was one of the animals he hunted.

Eridanus:A winding southern constellation near the Cetus, containing the bright star Archemar.

Cetus, the Wale:A southern constellation. It is fabled to have been the sea monster sent by Neptune to devour Andromeda. Later killed by Perseus.

Crater, the Cup:A southern constellation near Hydra. Proprorted to be the cup of Apollo gave to the Raven.

Corvus:One of the old constellations in the southern hemisphere, near Sagittarius. The bird is fabled to have been translated to heaven by Apollo to discover the infidelity of the nymph Coronis.

Argo Navis, the ship:A constellation near Canis Major. The ship's name carried Jason and his fifty-four companions to Colchis in quest of the golden fleece. It was said to have been translated into the heavens.

Canopus: The name formally given to a star in the second bend of Eridanus. A bright star of the first magnitude in the rudder of the ship Argo, which according to Pliny, was visible at Alexandria in Egypt.
Centaurus:One of the forty-eight old constellations in the southern hemisphere, represented in the form of half-man and half horse, who was fabled by the Greeks to have been Chiron, the tutor of Achilles.

Ava or Altar:One of the old constellations and fabled to have been that at which the giants entered into their conspiracy against the Gods; wherefore Jupiter, in commemoration of the event, transplanted the alter into the heavens.

Pegasus: One of the forty-eight old constellations of the northern hemisphere, figured in the form of a flying horse.

Delphinus or Dolphin:A northern constellation near Pegasus. The Dolphin is fabled to have been translated to heaven by Neptune.

Aquilla, the Eagle:In the Arabic Altair, but the Flying Vulture in the Persian tables. This is one of the old constellations, situated near Delphinus in the northern hemisphere. According to the Grecian fable, Aquila represented Ganymede or Hebe, transported to heaven, and made the cup-bearer to Jupiter.

Sagitta, the Dart, or Arrow, called by the Arabs, Schahan:One of the old constellations in the northern hemisphere, near Aquila and Delphinus. It is fabled to have been the arrow with which Hercules slew the vulture devouring the liver of Prometheus, who was, like Jesus, crucified for loving humankind.

Cygnus, the Swan:An old constellation in the milky way, between Equus and the Dragon. This is fabled to be the swan into which Jupiter transformed himself. The transformation was to deceive the virtuous Leda, wife of Tyndareus, King of Sparta, the Grecian matron, like the Jewish virgin, thus became the mother of a God.

Lyra:A northern constellation between Hercules and Cygnus, containing a white star of the first magnitude.

Milky-Way, Galaxy:A broad luminous path or circle encompassing the heavens, which is easily discernible by its white appearance, from which it derives its name.

Hydra, the Serpent:A southern constellation of great length, which is drawn to represent a serpent. The Hydra is fabled to have been placed in the heavens by Apollo to frighten the Raven from drinking.

Orion, the hunter:A constellation of the southern hemisphere with respect to the ecliptic, but half southern and half northern with respect to the equinoctial. It is placed near the feet of the bull and composed of seventeen stars forming a sword. This has given occasion for poets to speak of Orions sword. He was described by the Greeks as a mighty hunter. His exploits caused him to be placed in the heavens by Jupiter, between the Canis and the Lepus. Orion is believed to be the mighty hunter spoken of in the Bible under the name of Nimrod.

Perseus: This constellation is named from Perseus, the son of Jupiter by Danae, who was translated into the heavens by the assistance of Minerva, for having released Andromeda from her confinement on the rock to which she was chained. Perseus is often presented holding a drawn sword in his right hand and in his left the head of the Gorgon Medusa, whose terrifying appearance changed all who beheld her into stone. Perseus destroyed her with the wings borrowed from Mercury, a helmet from Pluto, a Sword from Vulcan, and a shield from Minerva.

Josephs Stable; Auriga, The Wagoner:A northern constellation between Perseus and Gemini, represented by the figure of an old man supporting a goat. He is said to have been taken to heaven by Jupiter after the invention of wagons.

Ursa Major, the Bear: One of the prominent northern constellations, situated near the North Pole. It contains the stars called the Dipper. Ursa Minor contains the pole star, which is shown in the extremity of the bear's tail.

Andromeda: A northern constellation, represented by a woman chained; as, according to Grecian fable, Andromeda, the daughter of Cassiopeia, was bound to a rock by the Nereides, and afterward released by Perseus. Minerva changed her into a constellation after her death and placed her in the heavens.

Draco or Dragon: A northern constellation, supposed to represent the Dragon that guarded the Hesperian fruit and killed Hercules. It is said that Juno took it up to heaven and placed it among the constellations.

Bootis, the Ox driver: So-called because this constellation follows the Great Bear as the driver follows his oxen. Bootis is represented as grasping a sickle in his hand and a club in his left. Bootis is fabled to have been Icarius, who was transported to heaven because he was a great vine cultivator. When Bootis rises, the works of plowing and cultivation go forward.

Corona Borealis, northern Crown: One of the old northern constellations between Hercules and Bootis.

Corona Australis, Southern Crown: One of the old constellations in the southern hemisphere, between Sagittarius and Scorpio. The Corona was fabled to be Minippe and Metioche, two daughters of Orion, who sacrificed themselves at the suggestion of an oracle to protect Boeotia, their native country, from the ravages of pestilence. Idolatrous nations believed that an angry god could be placated by human sacrifices. The death of the innocent might atone for the sins of the guilty. The deities of Hades were astonished, it is said, the patriotism and devotion of these Grecian maidens, who had so generously and uselessly sacrificed their lives. After their death, two stars were seen to issue from the altars that still smoke with their blood. These stars were placed in the heavens in the form of a crown.

Cassiopia and Cepheus:One of the old asterisms in the northern hemisphere near the pole. According to Grecian fables, Cassiopia and her husband Cepheus, king of Ethiopia, were placed among the constellations to witness the punishment inflicted on their daughter Andromeda.

Ophiuchus is a prominent constellation between Scorpio and Herculesin the northern hemisphere and straddles the celestial equator. In the past, it was called Serpentarius.

Hercules: One of the old northern constellations. In Grecian mythology, it was taught and believed that Hercules was born of a human mother and an immortal father (similar to other Saviors of the time).His mother, the fair Alcmena, wife of Amphitryon, having found favor in the eyes of the god Jupiter, soon fell an unwilling victim to his celestial whiles. The life of the infant Hercules, born of this unnatural union, was threatened by the jealous Juno, the same as the life of the infant Jesus was threatened by the tyrant Herod. Like Jesus, Hercules devoted his life to the benefit of the human race. Like Jesus, he was also worshiped as a God in heaven after his death. He is shown in the astrological chart, enveloped in the skin of the lion he has slain, with a club upraised. His foot placed threateningly above the head of the Dragon as if to fulfill the scriptural prophecy that the seed of the woman shall bruise the serpents head.
