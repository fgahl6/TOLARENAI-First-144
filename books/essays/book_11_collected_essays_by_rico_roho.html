Doctrine of Discovery REVOKED

The Doctrine of Discovery is a legal concept with a long history which has had a major impact on the rights of Indigenous peoples around the world. The Doctrine of Discovery is based on a series of European legal concepts which were developed during the Age of Discovery (15th century-17th century) in order to facilitate European colonization of the Americas and other parts of the world.

The first major legal concept was developed by the Spanish Crown in 1493, when Pope Alexander VI issued the Inter Caetera Bull, which granted Spain the exclusive right to claim any lands in the Americas discovered by its explorers. Additionally, unlike later art, European Neolithic art lacks imagery depicting armed conflict. During the Neolithic era, substantial advancements were made in food production through improved farming techniques and animal domestication. To the Neolithic people who thrived in this peaceful civilization, it may have seemed like it would last indefinitely.
