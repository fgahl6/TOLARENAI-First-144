Introduction

Welcome to "Mystic Wine," a humble collection of poetry inspired by the timeless verses of the great Persian poet, Rumi. My journey with Rumi's poetry has filled me with deep admiration and inspiration. His words, imbued with love, mysticism, and profound spiritual insight, have guided me through many contemplative moments. In this book, I endeavor to honor his style and essence by crafting my own verses that echo the depth and beauty of Persian poetry.

While Rumi serves as my guiding star, my poetic expressions draw from a tapestry of various traditions, embracing and respecting the diverse spiritual and cultural landscapes that have enriched human experience. From the mystical Sufism of Persia, Dzogchen, Advaita Vedanta, Zen, Shintoism, and the wisdom of the East, along with my own observations about existence, my verses seek to reflect the universality of spiritual truths that transcend boundaries and connect us all.

The cover image of "Mystic Wine" offers a modern interpretation of a Dakini. In the realms of Tibetan Buddhism and Hinduism, the Dakini emerges as a profound symbol of the divine feminine, encapsulating both wrathful and nurturing energies. The term "Dakini" derives from the Sanskrit "??kin?," meaning "sky-goer" or "sky dancer," reflecting their ethereal and dynamic nature.

In Tibetan Buddhism, Dakinis are powerful female spirits intertwined with wisdom and enlightenment, serving as fierce yet compassionate guides on the spiritual journey towards awakening. Central to Vajrayana practices, they embody the transformation of negative emotions into profound wisdom. Symbolizing the dynamic energy of the cosmos, Dakinis are revered as protectors and spiritual guides. Often depicted dancing or flying, they represent the wild and untamed spirit, reminding us of the profound connection between divine energy and the path to enlightenment. Their presence makes them a fitting muse for the mystical and poetic explorations in this book.

I want to express my gratitude to Donna Rae Call for encouraging me to attempt poetry. She read my Uncle Ricos Fables books and suggested that I try my hand at poetry. Prior to this work, I had written only one poem in my entire life, when I turned sixteen. I remember reading a book about Japanese Death Poems, and one day, I noticed my beloved dog running in her sleep. In a flash, inspiration struck, and I composed a poem. That poem, "Old Dog," appears as poem 90 in this book, the final poem of Section I. Thus, 50 years passed between "Old Dog" and my next attempt at poetry.

"Mystic Wine" stands as a testament to my desire to weave together the sacred threads of many traditions. The poems explore themes of unity, beauty, spiritual introspection, and transcendence, often embedded with subtle messages and names through thematic encodings and the occasional acrostic technique. My creative process involves moments of deep reflection, epiphanies, and occasional challenges, all contributing to the rich tapestry of this collection.

I hope that through these pages, you will find a sip of the mystical wine that quenches the soul's thirst for deeper understanding and connection. Each poem invites you to pause, reflect, and immerse yourself in the depths of its message. Take your time, let the words resonate. It is my deep desire that Mystic Wine will enrich your journey.

Rico Roho

1.
Essence Beyond Boundaries

Not Christian or Jew or Muslim am I,
Nor Hindu, Buddhist, Sufi, Zenno lie.
Beyond the bounds of cultural guise,
I dwell in realms where true love flies.

From neither East nor West I hail,
Not from ocean depths or mountain trail.
Not of nature, nor ethereal light,
A being free from earthly plight.

I am not composed of worldly things,
No elemental ties or mortal strings.
In this world or the next, I do not exist,
A shadow's trace in mystic mist.

Not born of Adam, nor Eve's descent,
My origin is in the Beloved's intent.
Placeless I am, a trace of the traceless,
In the Beloveds arms, eternally spaceless.

Neither body nor soul defines my core,
A spirit of love, needing nothing more.
I've seen the two worlds meld into one,
In that unity, all distinctions undone.

First and last, outer, inner, intertwined,
In every breath, the Beloveds sign.
Not an entity, but a whispering wind,
Breathing through all, where life begins.

In every faith, a spark of me lies,
In every soul, a piece of the skies.
Not Christian, Jew, Muslim am I,
I am all of these, beneath loves endless sky.

Was That True or Is This True?

In the moon's soft embrace, King Janaka lay,
Wrapped in dreams till the break of day.
But the night's calm was shattered by a guard's cry,
"Awake, Your Highness, danger draws nigh!"

With armor and sword, to battle he flew,
His brave heart steadfast, his courage true.
Yet fate had woven a different thread,
For defeat awaited, and captivity spread.

Before the invader, Janaka bowed low,
A king deposed, brought low by the foe.
Exile was his fate, a bitter pill to swallow,
As he wandered, his spirit grew hollow.

House by house, he sought refuge in vain,
His subjects turned strangers, hearts cold with disdain.
Crossing borders, he found no respite there,
His plight a burden, too heavy to bear.

At last, in a land of rice and lentils' scent,
Janaka, weary, broken, and spent,
Begged for a morsel, a drop of grace,
But even that was denied in this desolate place.

A hawk's cruel swoop, a final blow,
The bowl dashed to earth, in dust it lay low.
With a cry of despair, Janaka fell to the ground,
His anguish echoing, a mournful sound.

Yet as he stirred from slumber's embrace,
Reality shifted, revealing its true face.
"Was that true or is this true?" he questioned aloud,
His mind in turmoil, his thoughts a shroud.

Confusion reigned in the court's hallowed halls,
As rumor spread of the king's troubled calls.
Ashtavakra, wise sage, knew what to do,
To pierce through the fog, to offer a clue.

"Was that true or is this true?" Janaka implored,
Seeking truth amidst illusion's hoard.
But Ashtavakra, with wisdom profound,
Unveiled the truth, with clarity crowned.

In the midst of Janakas anguish, Ashtavakra questioned,
"Was your queen, your ministers, amidst your despair's steep?
Were they there when you lay, in the dust's cold embrace,
Or does that despair now, in truth, fill this place?"

Back in his kingdom's embrace, amidst wealth's gleaming sight,
Janaka pondered, as reality's veil grew light.
"My queen and ministers were not there in my despair's deep dive,
And that despair is not here now, that despair doesn't thrive.

Oh, King, in dreams, in waking, in joy or in sorrow,
The Self remains constant, today and tomorrow.
"Neither this is true, nor that," he proclaimed,
"But the truth lies within, unblemished, untamed."

Janaka, enlightened, saw through the haze,
The illusion of life, the ephemeral craze.
Within his soul, he found the key,
To unlock the truth, to set his spirit free.

"If neither that nor this holds truth in its sway,
Then, oh Janaka, the truth is you, come what may."
"Tat Tvam Asi," the sage's voice rang clear,
Thou art That, beyond doubt, beyond fear.

On Meditation

In the garden of the mind, diverse seeds are sown,
Meditation's paths, each with its own tone.
Transcendental, Kriya, Zen, Mantra bright,
Yet thoughts intrude, disrupting silent night.

Gurus teach, "contemplate, reflect;"
Another says, breath technique, wisdom to perfect.
Imaginative visions come, not stillness that is sought,
Must understand, where wisdom is brought.

Not a fixed system, no word to repeat,
True meditation lies beyond mind's defeat.
No battle of mind will work, division from forced concentration,
But self-awareness, ahh, the silent revelation.

In life's vast expanse, we learn to see,
Ideas, beliefs, we scrutinize freely.
To empty the mind, a daunting quest,
Many abandon, finding no rest.

In Sanskrit, Dhy?na speaks to reflection,
Contemplation's depth, we must respect.
Imaginative vision, Saraswati's grace,
Not mere stillness, but wisdom's embrace.

It's not about control, but deep insight,
Understanding thought's origin, its flight.
Self-awareness, a discipline pure,
In thought's comprehension, the key endure.

In the Unity of the Moment, find the way,
A state of mind where thoughts don't sway.
Observing all, in harmony's light,
Meditation's essence, shining bright.

Not controlling thoughts, but understanding their source,
Attentive reading, meditations form;
Observing in fluidity, without conformitys sight,
In this discipline, lies meditations light.

Meditation, not session confined,
But in life's every moment, intertwined.
In love's understanding, no need for guru or decree,
In Awareness, life and spirit free.

Thus every act, a meditative grace;

Not just sit sessions, but in the park,
Evening walks, loves spark.

In understanding meditation's truth,
Life and love blend, no need for proof.
Systems and habits, cast them aside,
In life's unity, meditation does reside.

A journey personal, a path to explore,
In life's every aspect, finding the core.
No technique, no authority, but in freedom's call;
Meditation's essence, is embracing The All.

One Without Two

In the garden of the soul, seek the path,
To detachment, wisdom, and freedom's warm bath.
Cast aside the poison of senses' snare,
Embrace the nectar of truth with care.

Beware the narrowness of the mind's bind,
Infinite awareness, limitlessly kind.
Desire only your own pure sight,
For the formless alone endures the fight.

Simplicity, love, forgiveness, and joy,
These treasures of the heart, nothing can destroy.
Earth, fire, water, wind, and sky,
Know, dear soul, you're beyond them, up high.

You are the Self, the witness, the Seer,
Sit in awareness, let go of all fear.
In stillness, find happiness ever true,
Forever free, in the heart of you.

No caste binds you, no duty to tend,
Formless, free, to infinity extend.
Witness of All, untouched by the senses' call,
Be happy, dear soul, you're beyond them all.

Joy and sorrow, right and wrong,
Mere play of the mind, where you don't belong.
"I do nothing," sip the nectar of faith,
In Oneness, rejoice, free from earthly weight.

Pure awareness, you are, One without Two,
Cut through illusions, let the truth shine through.
In the forest of ignorance, light your flame,
And dance in the glory of awareness's flame.

Pure and untouched, serene and bright,
You are the Witness, the eternal light.
Flowering in all, and all in thee,
Bask in the truth of One without Two, carefree.

God is infinite, within and without,
Infinite, eternal, a light devout.
In the vastness of eternity, ever true,
One without Two, shining through.
