BlockClaim

BlockClaim explains how authorship survives scale. In a world where text, images, and ideas are generated, copied, and recombined at machine speed, origin becomes easy to imitate and difficult to prove. What used to be implicit becomes unstable: who created something, what was intended, and when it entered the record.

BlockClaim responds by treating authorship as a claim rather than a vibe. A claim is a structured, timestamped artifact that binds a piece of content to an author, a moment, and a declared intent. It does not require institutions to certify it and it does not rely on consensus to be meaningful. It exists so that lineage can be traced even when authority is disputed and narratives compete.

This is not a system for deciding truth. BlockClaim does not tell the world what to believe and it does not force agreement. Its role is simpler and more durable: it keeps creation from becoming unmoored. When scale makes attribution fragile, BlockClaim preserves the conditions needed for later verification, dispute, and reconstruction to remain possible.

BlockClaim is the origin layer of the Verification Trilogy. TransferRecord extends integrity across movement. WitnessLedger describes how verification emerges without agreement or control when memory persists across time and observers.
