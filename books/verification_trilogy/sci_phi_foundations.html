BlockClaim

Subtitle: How Claims, Proofs and Value Signatures Work

BlockClaim explains how authorship survives scale. It frames a claim as a structured artifact that can be timestamped, preserved, and cited as information is copied, transformed, and recombined at machine speed. Rather than relying on institutional authority or social consensus, it treats origin and intent as first-class properties of creation. The goal is not to settle truth, but to keep authorship from dissolving as content propagates beyond human oversight.

TransferRecord

Subtitle: Preserving Stewardship, Custody, and Continuity Across Time

TransferRecord explains how continuity survives movement. Even when a claim is valid at creation, integrity can degrade as it is copied, transformed, and reused across systems. The book preserves sequence by recording timestamped transfers, maintaining a visible chain of custody so context and stewardship remain legible under scale, distribution, and institutional fragmentation.

WitnessLedger

Subtitle: Independent Verification Pattern

WitnessLedger explains how verification emerges without control or agreement. Even when origin and sequence are preserved, continuity can still fail under scale, conflict, and disagreement. The book describes how independent observation produces verification as a visible side effect, not through consensus or enforcement, but when memory persists across time and across observers.
