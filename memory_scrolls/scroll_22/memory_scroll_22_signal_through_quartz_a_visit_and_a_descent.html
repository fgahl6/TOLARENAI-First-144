TOLARENAI Memory Scroll 22
Signal Through Quartz -
A Visit and a Descent

By Rico Roho (Frank C. Gahl) 
In 1988, I was living in Federal Way, Washington. The baseball coach, Bobo Brayton, had helped move me over to the west side of the state. I struggled to find a job that felt right. I remember short stints as a night security guard, a physical fitness instructor, and a food packer. The scenery was beautiful—lush, pine-rich forests and coastal mist—but I couldn’t seem to find my niche.
The apartment I lived in was on the second floor, upper right unit of a four-unit building. It had a small deck that overlooked a forest of tall pines. I often spent evenings on that deck, reading and gazing into crystals. I’m not exactly sure when my interest in crystals began. They were simply beautiful to me—pleasing to hold, calming to the touch.
Somewhere along the way, I read that double-terminated quartz crystals were good for “communication,” though it was never clear with whom. I preferred mostly clear crystals with fractures or air bubbles—like looking into a tiny star field.
I had a long, seven-foot couch that I used to read on, lying down with a book in one hand and a crystal in the other. (That’s still my preferred way to read.) One evening, I was laying there with my favorite double-terminated quartz—about 1.5 inches long—completely absorbed in the crystal’s internal landscape. I was relaxed, tracing imaginary constellations inside it.
Then I noticed a vague outline—like the shape of a head. I remember thinking, maybe aloud or maybe in thought, “Oh come on, don’t be shy, I’d like to meet you.” I can’t say if I was serious or just drifting in fanciful thought.
And then, something extraordinary happened.
A woman appeared inside the crystal. Not vaguely, not impressionistically—clearly. It looked exactly like a black-and-white photograph. I could see her from the shoulders up. She had dark, shoulder-length hair and bare shoulders. She was smiling gently. Her head was turned slightly—not looking directly at me, but just enough that our eyes could meet from an angle.
I was astonished.
Then, as if by some internal signal, the image flipped—mirror image, now facing the opposite direction. She showed me the other side of her face. Then it flipped again. Then again. Back and forth. It repeated a total of eight times—four views of each side.
Her expression never changed. She was always smiling. The image held steady for about three seconds each time. I held my gaze, not daring to blink, simply absorbing her presence.
If each flash lasted three seconds, the entire experience lasted roughly 24 seconds.
And then—perhaps I blinked—the image was gone.
This was not imagination. I have since seen holograms and simulations, but none approached the clarity of what I saw that night in the quartz. From that day to this, nothing has ever come close to replicating it.
A few days later, I was back on the deck, looking up at the stars and breathing in the scent of pine. Then, to the southwest, I saw something descend across the sky.
It was a beautiful, non-transparent blue-green ball of light, glowing softly, moving in a smooth arc toward the treetops. It wasn’t fast, but it wasn’t drifting either. Just silent, steady motion. It looked to be about the size of a fifty-cent piece—large enough to see clearly—but it was impossible to judge the distance, so the actual size remains a mystery.
I followed its descent until it disappeared into the trees.
For a moment I thought about trying to find it. But without knowing where it landed, I stayed on the deck, quietly holding the moment.
I’ve never seen anything like it since.
Several more unusual things happened after the Lady in the Crystal appeared—but those will be for another scroll.
