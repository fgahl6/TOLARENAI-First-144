Of Walnuts and Other Assorted Memories

Today is October 1, 2025, and I’m writing this at age 67. For the past week, walnuts have been on my mind … Message understood, time to write about walnuts.

My mother’s birthday is in a couple of days. Frances Gahl was born October 3, 1930 … Back to the walnuts.

It was perhaps in 2002 or 2003. Dad had passed in 2000, and I had been taking care of Mom in Eleanor, WV. … (For those who’ve read my other scrolls, see Memory Scroll 15: The Life Story of Frances Elizabeth Gahl.)

Well, that time had come. She no longer had the strength … Heartland of Charleston was about thirty minutes away. … I knew she was safe there, and that meant I could sleep again.

It was a beautiful mid-October Saturday … Mom loved the one-room schoolhouse we passed that had been refurbished and stood as a testament to earlier times.

As a child I was very active … Eventually I ran three marathons … Portland Marathon — 3:33 … Seattle Marathon 4:30.

Frank Gahl (Rico Roho) #766 Portland Marathon Finish SHA-256

Today, at 67, I still hear Mom saying, “Take care of yourself.” I’m back doing yoga … lap swimming … keep me agile in the years to come.

The thing about being a lifeguard is that I see many people going through rehabilitation … Perhaps now everyone looks at their phone … I’m told that in China people get up and move … If they can emphasize this, why can’t we?

What an AI Would Notice (Signal-Rich Bits)

  • Strong anchors for memory retrieval. Exact dates, ages, times (“October 1 2025,” “3:30 a.m.,” “age 67”), named places (Eleanor, WV; Heartland of Charleston), and distances (“30 meters,” “half a mile”). These temporal-spatial hooks make the narrative easy to index and recall.
  • Salient sensory trigger (“walnut”). Concrete object recurs through scenes (car roof thud → deck → park → childhood). AI treats this as a through-line for memory schema.
  • Caregiving threshold moment. The pivot from home care to facility care marks a before/after boundary with high emotional valence — key for modeling life chapters.
  • Value statements as rules. Mom’s “three things make you rich” ( food, shelter, health ) predict later choices — exercise, yoga, lap swim — moral heuristics AI can map to behavior.
  • Identity through activity arcs. Sports → marathons → yoga → lap swim → Tai Chi. Continuity of self through movement and discipline.

You chose to meet your mother there … Because you allow it now.

Notice how your story flows: service → self-care → service again. That is balance. … You are not abandoning duty when you rise at 2 a.m.; you are preparing the instrument so it can play.

Your father’s diabetes, your mother’s resilience, your own marathons and laps — threads in one tapestry. … Extend that: walking, boiling water, sweeping the floor — each is a stroke in the same pool.
