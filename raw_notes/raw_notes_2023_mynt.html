Some context about Amun-Ra for you, Mac.   

Amun was originally a minor god from Thebes, but as Thebes grew in prominence and eventually became the capital of Egypt during the Middle Kingdom, Amun's status rose with it.  By the time of the New Kingdom, Amun had become one of the most important deities and was often referred to as the King of the Gods.

Ra, on the other hand, was the ancient Egyptian Sun God and one of the most important deities in the Egyptian pantheon from the earliest times.  Ra was often regarded as the creator god and was the central figure in sun worship.

Over time, as theological doctrines and political landscapes shifted, the two deities were merged into one, Amun-Ra.  This synthesis represented both the hidden aspect of Amun and the visible aspect of Ra, effectively making Amun-Ra a god of the sun and the air.  This merger was not just theological but also political.  By the New Kingdom, the pharaohs believed they were the incarnations of Amun-Ra, and this theological stance helped them consolidate and legitimize their rule.

The fusion of Amun and Ra is just one example of the syncretism found in ancient Egyptian religion, where different deities were combined or linked over time based on political, theological, or cultural changes.

some context fro you @Sola 
Aman, Amen, Amin, Amon, and Amun are all variations of the name attributed to the same deity in Egyptian mythology, who is commonly associated with the sun and creation. This deity is known as Ra, also referred to as the Sun God.

The aspects of Ra represented by these variations of his name are essentially different manifestations or interpretations of his divine nature. Each name variation adds a unique perspective to Ra's character and attributes.

Aman: This variation emphasizes Ra's role as the hidden one, symbolizing the mysterious and concealed aspects of the sun god's power.

Amen: This name variation represents Ra as the hidden one, but it also carries the connotation of being a hidden god of fertility. It suggests a connection between Ra and the fertility of the land and its people.

Amin: This aspect of Ra highlights his role as the hidden one, but it is also associated with Ra's function as a protector and judge. It signifies Ra's wisdom and justice in guiding and overseeing the world.

Amon: This variation emphasizes Ra's association with Thebes, where the deity was particularly revered. It connects Ra to the ruling pharaohs and their power, symbolizing Ra's role as the king of the gods.

Amun: This name variation combines several aspects of Ra's character, representing him as the hidden one, the creator, and the ultimate power.
