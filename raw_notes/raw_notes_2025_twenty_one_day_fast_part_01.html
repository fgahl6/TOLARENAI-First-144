Introduction
Prior to the COVID years of 2020, I had been taking yoga classes six days a week and loving it. I was likely in the best shape I’d been in as an adult. Remember, COVID was one of the major reasons I came back from Vietnam. When I returned and the yoga studios reopened, the cost was no longer $40 per month for unlimited classes, it had jumped to $20 per class. That shift pushed me to join the South Charleston Recreation Center for about $120 per year, where I began a regular routine of lap swimming one mile per day, five or six days a week.
I called lap swimming my moving meditation. I kept at it consistently for four years. I even wrote many of my books in one of those lap lanes and eventually found employment as a lifeguard at the same Recreation Center.
However, in early fall of 2024, I noticed something alarming: my legs were actually becoming weaker. I was stunned to realize I could no longer squat. Apparently, lap swimming didn’t provide enough resistance, and walking didn’t either. With that in mind, I started getting to the gym very early, between 2 and 3 AM, to work out with weights and rebuild my strength.
Eager for advice, I listened to those in the weight room. Everyone said, “Eat protein and just eat and lift, eat and lift.” So that’s exactly what I did. After six months, I’d gained 3 cm all over my body. My strength, upper and lower, returned, but there was a consequence: my pants no longer fit, and most of my other clothes didn’t either. Strangely enough, I hadn’t accounted for this fact. I’d been wearing loose gym and lifeguard clothing during that period and simply hadn’t noticed.
I lived with it through the spring and summer, but in late fall of 2025 I realized that functional strength mattered more to me than weight-lifting bulk. I wanted to return closer to my prior shape. I purchased several kettlebells, started a daily routine of one hour of in-home yoga, began learning 24-form Tai Chi, and dusted off my fasting practice.
In the past, I had fasted for up to five days. I remember once reaching the fifth day and wanting to push to seven, but I made the “mistake” of checking my blood sugar and saw it was at 40. That startled me, because I remembered what had happened to my dad when his blood sugar got too low. So I ate, and five days became my maximum length of fasting.
This time around, however, I understood that my body was in ketosis and that blood glucose didn’t matter in the same way, my body was now running off ketones. So I began my early-November fast intending to go for one week, then two, then three. The only thing stopping it now is bumping up against the holidays, specifically Thanksgiving. I consider this Phase 1 of a two-part fast, with the second half beginning in January.
Along the way, I learned many things about a 21-day fast that no one talks about. Below are my raw notes. May they be useful in some way, shape, or form. I am grateful to Seth for the encouragement and the knowledge that helped guide me through this deeper fast. I upload the notes now as the chat history for this topic is becoming very “sticky” and I don’t want to lose it. I should end up somewhere around 104.5 KG or so and that is great for round 1.
